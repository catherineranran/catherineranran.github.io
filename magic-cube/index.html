Basics

Before you start

The pieces, the color scheme and how to read the moves.

The pieces

  • 24 centersFour per face. Nothing holds them in place, so you build each center yourself.
  • 24 edgesTwo per edge. Each pair has the same two colors.
  • 8 cornersThey behave just like the corners of a 3×3.

The color scheme

No center is fixed, so the cube can't show you where each color belongs. Use the standard scheme:

  • White is opposite yellow
  • Green is opposite blue
  • Red is opposite orange

Hold white on top and green in front: red is on the right, orange on the left and blue at the back. Building the centers in the wrong order makes the cube impossible to finish, so check this before you start stage 1.

Reading the moves

RLUDFB
Right, left, up, down, front, back. Turn that face a quarter turn clockwise, as if you were looking straight at it.
R'
A prime means counterclockwise.
R2
A half turn. Either direction works.
Rw
Wide: the two right layers together. Uw, Fw, Lw, Dw and Bw work the same way.
r
Only the second layer from the right, also written 2R.
xyz
Turn the whole cube in your hands: x follows R, y follows U, z follows F.

R U R'Tap a move to see it on the cube.

1

Centers

Build six solid 2×2 centers.

A 4×4 has 24 center pieces, four of each color, and they move around freely. Gather each color into a 2×2 block. Edges and corners don't matter yet, so they're grey in the examples, and the flat map under each cube shows all six faces at once.

1.1White center

Build white on top. Wide turns do the work: Rw, Lw, Fw and Bw each carry a column of center pieces from one face to the next.

  • Lift white pieces onto the top with a wide turn.
  • Before you lift, turn the top (U) so the white pieces already there aren't in the column that's about to move.
  • Make a 1×2 bar first, then a second bar, then join them.

1.2Yellow center, opposite white

Turn the cube over (x2) so white is on the bottom. From now on, any slice you turn has to be turned back, or you'll break the white center. This three-move insert only swaps pieces between the top and the front, so white is never touched:

  1. Turn the whole cube (y) until a yellow piece is on the front.
  2. Turn F until that piece sits at the bottom-right of the front center.
  3. Turn U until the back-left spot of the top center is empty (not yellow).
  4. Do Rw U Rw'. The piece lands at the front-left of the top.

1.3The third center

Hold the cube with white on the left and yellow on the right. Rw and Lw only move the top, front, bottom and back, so they can't disturb white or yellow. Build green on top exactly the way you built white, using Rw, Lw and turns of U, F, D and B.

1.4The fourth center

Tilt the cube (x) so green goes to the back. Orange goes on top now: with white on the left, yellow on the right and green at the back, the top of a solved cube is orange. Insert orange pieces with the same rule as before, gap at the back-left of the top:

  • From the front: piece at the front's bottom-right, then Rw U Rw'.
  • From the bottom: piece at the back-right of the bottom center, then Rw2 U Rw2.

Both leave white, yellow and green alone.

1.5The last two centers

Tilt the cube again (x). Green is now on the bottom and orange at the back, so blue belongs on top and red in front. Keep inserting blue pieces from the front with Rw U Rw' until the top is all blue. The front turns red by itself.

2

Edges

Pair the 24 edge pieces into 12 double edges.

Each edge of a 4×4 is two pieces with the same two colors. Your job is to get every matching pair side by side. One algorithm does it, and it puts the centers back when it's done. In the examples the corners are grey, and only the two pieces being paired are in color.

2.1Pair two pieces

  1. Find two edge pieces with the same two colors.
  2. Put one in the front-right edge and the other in the front-left edge, both in the bottom half. They'll show the same color toward you. Use outer turns for this (R, L, U, D, F, B), which never move the centers.
  3. Do Dw R U R' F R' F' R Dw'. The left piece moves over and joins its partner on the right.
  4. Move the finished pair into the top or bottom layer, for example with R U R'. Pairs are safe there.

2.2When they're at different heights

If one piece is in the top half and the other in the bottom half, flip the right edge first with R U R' F R' F' R. Those are the same seven moves as in the middle of the pairing algorithm: they swap the top and bottom halves of the front-right edge and leave the front-left edge alone. If both pieces are in the top half, F2 drops them both to the bottom.

2.3The last two edges

Pairing always leaves a spare piece behind in the front-left edge, so you finish with two unpaired edges. Put them in the front-left and front-right edges. If the two bottom pieces match each other, one pairing algorithm closes both edges at once. If they don't, flip the right edge first, as in 2.2.

3

Solve it like a 3×3

Outer turns only from here on.

Treat every 2×2 center as one center and every pair as one edge, and turn only the outer layers (R, L, U, D, F, B). If you can already solve a 3×3, do that now and keep stage 4 handy. If not, here's a beginner's method with white on the bottom and yellow on top.

3.1White cross

First make a daisy: get all four white edges onto the top around the yellow center, white facing up. If a white edge is in the middle layer, turn its side face to bring it up, but turn U first so you don't knock off a petal you already have.

Then, for each petal, turn U until its side color matches the center below it, and turn that face twice. It drops into place on the bottom.

3.2White corners

Find a white corner in the top layer. Turn U until it sits above its spot, between the two centers that match its other colors, and hold the cube so that spot is at the front-right. Repeat R U R' U' until the corner drops in the right way round. That takes 1, 3 or 5 repeats.

A white corner stuck in the bottom layer in the wrong place: hold it at the front-right and do R U R' U' once to bring it up.

3.3Middle layer

Find an edge in the top layer without any yellow. Turn U until its front color matches the front center, then check its top color:

  • Matches the right center: U R U' R' U' F' U F
  • Matches the left center: U' L' U L U F U' F'

An edge in the middle layer but wrong: insert any top edge into its slot to pop it out, then insert it properly.

3.4Yellow cross

Look only at the yellow edges on top and do F R U R' U' F' as often as you need:

  • Dot: do it once to get an L.
  • L: hold the L at the back-left and do it to get a line.
  • Line: hold it left to right and do it to get the cross.

One or three yellow edges? A 3×3 can't do that, so it's parity. Fix it with step 4.1, then come back.

3.5Yellow face

Count the yellow corners on top, hold the cube as below, and do R U R' U R U2 R', known as the Sune. Repeat until the top is all yellow.

  • None: turn U until the front-left corner shows yellow on its left side.
  • One: turn U to bring it to the front-left.
  • Two: turn U until the front-left corner shows yellow facing you.

3.6Yellow corners

Look at the sides of the top layer for two corners with the same color on one side. Cubers call them headlights. Hold the headlights at the back and do R' F R' B2 R F' R' B2 R2. No headlights anywhere? Do it once from any side, and they'll appear. Finish by turning U to line the corners up with the centers.

3.7Yellow edges

Hold a fully solved side at the back and do R U' R U R U R U' R' U' R2. If the edges still aren't right, do it once more. No solved side? Do it once from anywhere, then look again.

Two edges need to swap and nothing else? That's the other kind of parity: see step 4.2.

4

Parity

Two positions a 3×3 never shows you.

Pairing edges can leave the 4×4 in a position no 3×3 can reach. You didn't make a mistake: it comes from the way the edges happened to pair up. Each case has one fix, and about three solves in four need at least one of them.

4.1OLL parity: one flipped edge

When you'll see it: at the yellow cross, there are one or three yellow edges on top. Hold the cube with a non-yellow edge at the front and do:

Rw U2 x Rw U2 Rw U2 Rw' U2 Lw U2 Rw' U2 Rw U2 Rw' U2 Rw'

It flips that edge and leaves the first two layers alone. The rest of the top layer gets mixed up, which doesn't matter: go back to the yellow cross. The x halfway through means turn the whole cube like an R move, then carry on.

4.2PLL parity: two swapped edges

When you'll see it: at the very end, two edges need to swap and everything else is solved. Hold the two edges at the front and back and do:

r2 U2 r2 Uw2 r2 Uw2, then U2

r2 turns only the second layer from the right; Uw2 turns the top two layers. If the two edges are next to each other instead, do the algorithm and U2 from any side, then finish with step 3.7.
