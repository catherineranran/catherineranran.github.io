First, what is psychology?

Before we turn to ourselves,
let’s look at a map of the discipline.

01

A map of the field · 3 MIN

What are the branches of psychology?

It studies cognition and emotion, and also personality, development, social relationships, and working life.

Show a short overview ＋

Basic fields: cognitive, developmental, biological and neuropsychology, personality, social, and cross-cultural psychology.

Applied fields: clinical and counselling, educational, industrial and organisational, and health psychology, as well as human–computer interaction.

Research methods and statistics run through every field; positive psychology looks at well-being, meaning, and human strengths.

02

Understanding patterns, responding to people · 3 MIN

How do counselling and
psychological research relate?

Research looks for general patterns of the mind; counselling attends to a particular person. Through evidence and practice, each strengthens the other.

Show a short overview ＋

Research proposes and tests explanations through questionnaires, experiments, and observing behaviour.

Counselling makes sense of difficulties within a professional relationship, combining research evidence, professional experience, and the client’s needs.

A PhD in psychology and a licence to practise counselling are different training paths. This lecture takes the research perspective.

03

Different paths to understanding and change · 4 MIN

What are the main schools
of counselling?

How different approaches make sense of people’s difficulties, and what each of them focuses on.

Show a short overview ＋

Cognitive-behavioural: how thoughts, emotions, and behaviour interact. Psychodynamic: recurring patterns in experience and relationships.

Humanistic and existential: acceptance, free choice, and meaning. Systemic and family: patterns of interaction and networks of relationships.

In practice, approaches are often combined; each school, each specific method, and the problems it suits deserve to be looked at on their own.

10—2515 minChapter 1 · Personality and motivationSee yourself: What kind of person am I?＋

The Big Five and HEXACO; the MBTI, Jung’s eight cognitive functions, and self-exploration; the Dark Triad as personality traits. Combining self-reports, reports from others, and everyday behaviour to understand what measurement means.

From Maslow’s theory of needs to the needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness in self-determination theory.

Think about it: are you the same person when you are alone, with friends, and at work?
25—3712 minChapter 2 · Situations, culture, and organisationsSee the world: How does my environment shape me?＋

How do personality and situations shape behaviour together? From situational strength and trait activation to the environments we actively choose and shape.

Cross-cultural psychology: individualism and collectivism, and the differences within a culture. Organisational psychology: personality and ability assessment, person–job fit, and 360-degree feedback.

Think about it: which environments make it easier for you to be your true self?
37—4811 minChapter 3 · Social influence and prosocial behaviourSee others: How does caring become action?＋

Revisiting Asch’s conformity studies, Milgram’s obedience studies, and the bystander effect; using the Stanford Prison Experiment to discuss research evidence and methodological controversy.

Understanding cooperation through the prisoner’s dilemma, and responsibility, risk, and helping through bystander situations; and seeing how group interaction affects creativity.

Think about it: when helping comes at a cost, what would make you take the first step?
48—557 minA window on methods · Research and new technologyHow do we know all this?＋

Questionnaires and experiments, economic decision games, diaries and experience sampling, longitudinal studies: different methods answer different questions.

Understanding correlation and causation, effect sizes, and uncertainty. A look into VR behavioural research and human–computer interaction: how do immersion, AI-generated scenarios, and real-world behaviour relate?

Think about it: can “I say I would help” and “I actually helped” be measured in the same way?
55—605 minDiscussion and looking backBack to life, with better questions.＋

Bringing psychological mechanisms, lived experience, and reflection on values into one conversation: once we understand what drives behaviour, how do we think about choice, responsibility, and kindness?

Take one observation home: something you have discovered about yourself, a situation you would like to change, or an action you are willing to try.
